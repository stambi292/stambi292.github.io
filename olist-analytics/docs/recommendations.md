# Business Recommendations: Olist Marketplace

**Scope:** 98,207 valid orders from 94,990 customers, January 2017 to August 2018. R$15.7M revenue (item price plus freight).

**Summary:** Olist grew revenue 140% year over year (Jan–Aug 2018 vs. Jan–Aug 2017), but monthly revenue has been flat at about R$1.0–1.15M since January 2018. Growth so far has come almost entirely from new customers: only 3.0% of customers ever buy again. The clearest lever on customer satisfaction is on-time delivery. The five recommendations below are ordered by expected impact.

---

### 1. Make on-time delivery the top operational priority

- Orders that arrive on or before the promised date get a 1–2 star review about **9–12%** of the time. Orders 1–3 days late get one **32%** of the time, 4–7 days late **68%**, and 8+ days late **79%**.
- Late deliveries are only 6.8% of delivered orders, but they account for **32%** of all 1–2 star reviews.
- Lateness spikes in some months: **12.4%** of orders were late in November 2017 (Black Friday) and **14–19%** in February–March 2018, against under 8% in every other month.

**Actions:** Agree on-time targets with carriers and track them weekly. Add carrier and seller capacity before known peaks. Message customers when an order is going to be late, before the promised date passes.

### 2. Start a retention program

- **3.0%** of customers placed a second order, and repeat buyers account for only 5.7% of revenue.
- Of those second orders, 30% happened the same day as the first (a split basket, not a return visit), so real return visits are even rarer.
- Customers who return on a later day take a median of **75 days**; the quickest quarter come back within 23 days.

**Actions:** Send follow-up offers at about 3 weeks and again at about 2 months after purchase, timed to when returning customers naturally come back. Recommend products from related categories. Measure it against a holdout group so the lift is real, not assumed.

### 3. Protect the first order

- Customers whose **first** order arrived late came back **3.05%** of the time, against **4.09%** when it arrived on time. That is about 25% fewer returning customers.

**Actions:** Prioritize first-time buyers in fulfillment (faster shipping options, priority handling). Contact first-time buyers proactively after any delay, for example with a small credit.

### 4. Fix the Northeast delivery gap

| Region | Avg delivery | Late orders | Freight share of order | Avg review |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| São Paulo | 8.7 days | 4.5% | 12.2% | 4.21 |
| Northeast | 19.9 days | 12.7% | 17.9% | 3.92 |

- The Northeast is 9.5% of orders and the region with the highest late rate. Most sellers are in São Paulo state, so these orders travel the farthest.

**Actions:** Recruit sellers based in the Northeast, starting with the top-selling categories. Evaluate a regional fulfillment hub. Test a freight discount there to see whether lower shipping cost raises conversion enough to pay for itself.

### 5. Handle orders from multiple sellers better

- Orders with items from more than one seller get a 1–2 star review **47%** of the time, against 13% for single-seller orders. They are 1.3% of orders, but the gap is very large.
- These items usually ship as separate packages, so customers may think their order arrived incomplete.

**Actions:** Tell customers at checkout that items will arrive in separate packages. Show tracking for each package. Ask for the review only after the last package arrives.

---

**Caveats.** These findings are correlations from historical data, not controlled experiments. For example, late orders might also differ in product type or region. Each action above should be tested (A/B test or a pilot region) before a full rollout. Partial months (2016 and September–October 2018) are excluded from trends.
