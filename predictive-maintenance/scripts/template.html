Fix it before it fails

Service a machine too early and you throw away good life; too late and it breaks in the field. I trained a machine learning model on 100 jet engines that were run until they failed, taught it to read 15 sensors and predict how many cycles each engine has left, and priced what that is worth against a fixed service schedule.

Independent analysis by Stephen tAMBI on the public NASA C-MAPSS dataset. Not affiliated with NASA or any manufacturer.

Watching an engine wear out

For engines it never saw while learning, the model predicts the remaining life at every cycle. Early on it only knows the engine is healthy; as the sensors drift, the prediction locks on to the real countdown.

How it was built

  • Data:
  • Features: each sensor's value, its 10-cycle average and its 20-cycle trend (slope), so the model sees both the level and the direction of wear.
  • Models: linear regression, random forest and gradient boosting in Python with scikit-learn, trained on the 100 run-to-failure engines and tested on 100 separate engines whose true remaining life is published.
  • Policies: run-to-failure; a fixed service interval; and predictive service when the predicted remaining life falls to the safety margin (from cycle 30 on). Each service or failure resets the engine.
Data: NASA Prognostics Center of Excellence, Turbofan Engine Degradation Simulation Data Set (C-MAPSS), sub-dataset FD001: A. Saxena, K. Goebel, D. Simon and N. Eklund, 2008.
