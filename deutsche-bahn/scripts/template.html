On track?How Deutsche Bahn got back into profit, how it counts time, and why people still ride

In the first half of 2026 Deutsche Bahn made a profit for the first time in seven years, and it carries more passengers than ever. But its long-distance trains are less punctual than in any recent year. The numbers behind the turnaround, and behind the trust problem.

The trust problem: time

How Deutsche Bahn keeps track of time

Every train run is logged twice: the timetable says when it should arrive, the network's operating systems record when it did. DB compares the two for every run, and the same live data feeds the departure boards, the DB Navigator app and outside developers.

< 6 min

A train is "on time" if it arrives less than six minutes late. Five minutes and 59 seconds counts as punctual.

Cancelled = invisible

Cancelled trains are not in the punctuality figure. In 2025 Der Spiegel reported delayed trains being cancelled to protect the statistic; DB denied it.

DB also publishes "whole-journey" punctuality, which counts passengers arriving on time even if they changed trains. It is always higher.

Checked from outside

Railwise analysed over 180,000 long-distance arrivals for Handelsblatt using DB's own licensed timetable interface. DB said the gap was "not traceable" and that its figures meet its quality checks.
