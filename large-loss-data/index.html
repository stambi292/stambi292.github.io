Large Loss Data Office

The work of a reinsurance data office in one page: collect large-loss events, validate them before anyone uses them, report them by peril and year, and see what they mean for an excess-of-loss layer. Built on NOAA's public list of 403 US billion-dollar disasters, 1980–2024.

    3. What it means for a reinsurance layer

    A per-event excess-of-loss layer pays the part of each event's insured loss above the attachment, up to the limit. Only a share of economic loss is insured, so set that share first. The perils and years chosen above apply here too.

    Paid by the layer, $bn per year

    Illustrative: NOAA counts economic losses, not insured losses, and US events only. A real data office would use the entities' reported insured and ceded losses.

    Method and sources

    • Data: NOAA NCEI, U.S. Billion-Dollar Weather and Climate Disasters, events 1980–2024 (the final release), losses in millions of US dollars, CPI-adjusted to 2024.
    • More events per year partly reflects more property in harm's way and the fixed $1bn threshold, not only more extreme weather.
    • Layer maths: layer loss = min(limit, max(0, insured loss − attachment)) per event, with insured loss = economic loss × insured share.
